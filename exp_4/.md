## Something about this:

A differential amplifier is an essential analog circuit used to amplify the difference between two input signals while rejecting noise that is common to both inputs.

Due to this property, it is widely used in:

Operational amplifiers

Analog signal processing systems


It provides high noise immunity and stable operation.


---

## explaination:

A CMOS differential amplifier consists of:

Two matched MOS transistors

A common source node

A tail current source


The differential input is defined as:

V_d = V_{in1} - V_{in2}

Operation:

If  increases → current through M1 increases

If  increases → current through M2 increases


For small input signals:

Both transistors operate in saturation region

The circuit behaves linearly



---

Small Signal Gain:

A_v = g_m * R_{out}

Where:

 = transconductance

 = output resistance


And:

g_m = {2I_D}/{V_{ov}}

## Circuit 2: Differential Amplifier with PMOS Active Load

---

## Working Principle

The given circuit is composed of three main parts.  
NMOS transistors M1 and M2 form the differential pair.  
PMOS transistors M3 and M4 act as active loads.  
NMOS transistor M5 acts as the tail current source.  

When a differential input is applied, the tail current provided by M5 is divided between M1 and M2 depending on the input voltages.  

An increase in Vin1 causes M1 to conduct more current, while an increase in Vin2 causes M2 to conduct more current.  

The PMOS active load converts these current variations into voltage variations at the output nodes.  

The output is taken from the drains of M1 and M2.  

Due to the use of active load, the output resistance increases significantly, which results in higher gain.

## Power Constraint

Total supply voltage is calculated as  

VDD − VSS = 0.9 − (−0.9) = 1.8 V  

Using power relation  

P = (VDD − VSS) × Iss  

Substituting values  

1.8 × Iss ≤ 1.8 mW  

Iss ≤ 1 mA  

Thus, the chosen value is  

Iss = 1 mA  

---

## Drain Current Distribution

Under balanced input condition  

Vin1 = Vin2  

The tail current splits equally between both branches  

ID1 = 0.5 mA  
ID2 = 0.5 mA  

This confirms symmetrical operation of the differential amplifier.

---

## Bias Point (Initial Understanding)

At equilibrium condition  

Vin1 = Vin2 = 0 V  
Source node Vs = -0.7 V  

Gate Source Voltage  

Vgs = Vg − Vs = 0 − (−0.7) = 0.7 V  

Overdrive Voltage  

Vov = Vgs − Vth = 0.7 − 0.36 = 0.34 V  

This ensures that transistors operate in saturation region.

---

## Bias Completion

### NMOS Transistors M1 and M2

Drain voltage Vd = 0 V  
Source voltage Vs = -0.7 V  

Vds = Vd − Vs = 0 − (−0.7) = 0.7 V  

Overdrive voltage Vov = 0.34 V  

Condition  

Vds ≥ Vov → 0.7 ≥ 0.34  

Hence M1 and M2 operate in saturation region.

---

### NMOS Tail Current Source M5

Source voltage Vs = -0.9 V  
Drain voltage Vd = -0.7 V  

Vds = -0.7 − (−0.9) = 0.2 V  

For saturation  

Vds ≥ Vov  

Choose  

Vov ≈ 0.2 V  

Gate voltage calculation  

Vgs = Vth + Vov = 0.36 + 0.2 = 0.56 V  

Vg = Vs + Vgs = -0.9 + 0.56 = -0.34 V  

Thus M5 operates at edge of saturation and sets the tail current.

---

### PMOS Load M3 and M4

Source voltage Vs = 0.9 V  
Drain voltage Vd = 0 V  

Vsd = 0.9 V  

Condition  

Vsd ≥ Vov  

Hence PMOS transistors operate in saturation.

---

## Final Bias Summary

M1 and M2 operate in saturation  
M3 and M4 operate in saturation  
M5 operates at edge of saturation  

Thus proper differential operation is achieved.

---
